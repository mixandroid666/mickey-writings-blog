At certain times in life, at an age when stress comes from work and responsibility rather than homework or teenage love, he would take himself to a place called a school, pretending he had returned to his high school days.

The familiar sights, tastes, smells, sounds, and atmosphere made it easy to return to memories that were still clear. He would play the songs he had discovered back then to make this illusion feel more real, wandering along the paths around the school and humming like a man who had lost his mind.

It was a little after five. Some students were still playing football or basketball, sitting in groups and talking, or playing guitar and singing in different corners of the school. But many kept a smartphone in their hands at all times. Some took it out to use while they talked. They moved between two worlds without much effort.

The light of the setting sun reminded him of himself and his friends. They had done much the same things. We were happy with simple things, and the stress and pressure were nowhere near what they are now. Many songs he had “discovered” with his friends, or felt “cool” listening to, had become his own personal “classics,” even if they had never been popular hits. High school really was a time when we gathered the raw materials from which to build ourselves and our egos.

He had found this way of healing himself three or four years into his working life. It was an age when he began to have “importance,” becoming one of the main moving parts in a larger body called a “company.” People expected things of him and placed their trust in him. This responsibility came in exchange for more of the thing called “money,” which all human beings had agreed to use as a “medium of exchange” or a “unit of economic value.”

We cannot help being tied to it. We need it. It is our master. Its power is not so strong while we are still students. But it shows its might and claims its rule over us when we begin doing things to earn it ourselves. In short, when we begin “working.”

More than ten years passed. After a long time away, he returned to this place once again. It was the same time of day. He hoped this illusion would heal his heart once again. But this time was different. The feeling no longer came. Perhaps time had piled up until these precious memories were buried forever. He knew they existed, but he could not find them. No matter which song he played, or how hard he tried to recall the face of the girl in his class he had been so taken with, he could no longer dig up this magic medicine. It had simply vanished. The sunlight falling on his face no longer meant anything. It was only a cycle, as it had always been. The students’ activities, which he had once filled with lovely meanings, were now only ordinary children waiting to grow up. Worse still, he felt sorry for those who might grow up and go through what he had.

“Bzz, bzz.”

His phone vibrated in his trouser pocket.

“The work we agreed to deliver on Monday—the client wants it tomorrow instead. Could you take care of it?”

He read it with trembling hands and a racing heart. He let out a deep sigh and headed back to his room to finish the work.

Behind him, on the field in the middle of the school, students were still playing football and basketball. Boys and girls still sat talking on the benches. In some corner of the toilets, a group of boys was probably sneaking a smoke and making dirty jokes, as boys do.

But he was no longer there.
