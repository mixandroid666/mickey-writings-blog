---
title: "The First Companion"
number: 11
excerpt: "A quiet companion walks beside us throughout life, reminding us how brief and precious each moment, love, and memory can be."
language: en
---

The companions around us come in many kinds and forms. The friends we have reflect who we are and can tell others something about us. There are childhood friends who grew up with us and liked the same cartoons or games. There are friends from high school or university who shared our taste in music and fashion. In our working years, there are friends who share our views of the world or the values we hold. But there is one companion who visits us every year without us even knowing. Once we meet his eyes or are properly introduced, we realize that he has been too close for us to see. Every year, he comes quietly, waves, calls out a greeting, and slips away. Yet it takes us years, sometimes decades, to notice that he is there.

This companion has been watching us since the day we were born. Strangely, as children, we cannot sense his presence at all. Perhaps we have too much to learn and too many things to play with. Every childhood birthday is full of joy. Our parents and the adults around us make much of our birthday celebrations. We receive so much love and warmth from them. Perhaps this companion is shy, especially around large groups of people. He rarely shows himself during those early years of our lives.

As time passes, we enter our working years. Many of us lose touch with, or forget, old friends from childhood, high school, or university. We focus on the goals we have set for ourselves: money, careers, and starting a family. We give our flesh and blood, our sweat, and our time to this struggle. Some fight on alone, firmly and willingly, telling themselves that this is happiness and that one day they will surely succeed.

There is good news for all those who struggle: we still have one companion watching over us! He is waiting for something from us. More importantly, we seem to become more aware of his presence with every birthday—or perhaps with the passing of some other anniversary. We begin to hear him calling out, or perhaps we only begin to realize that he has been calling. We start to see him wave, even when he stands far away. At first, we may be afraid or feel threatened. That is because we do not know him very well. But once we open our hearts and speak to him, or return his greeting, we begin to sense his kindness and good intentions.

Once, I found myself unable to breathe in my sleep. I was aware of what was happening, but I could not move. In that moment, I was filled with shock and fear. Yet this companion was there beside me, sitting so close that we almost touched. Sometimes he said nothing and simply stood there. When I jolted awake, he was gone, leaving me gasping for breath and soaked in sweat.

When my birthday came around again, I found that his voice had grown louder, and I could begin to make out what he was trying to say. Perhaps it was also because I was making an effort to listen. I saw him waving in the distance, trying to walk toward me with a smile on his face. I could sense his kindness, though he said very little. After we had been properly introduced, he began to appear more often. His visits were no longer tied to any particular time. Sometimes he appeared when I woke up, while I was taking a shower, or even while I was working. When I was surrounded by people, this companion seemed to have learned to fit in. He appeared among them with little effort. Before I knew it, he was always somewhere in my sight—sometimes far away, sometimes close, whenever the chance came.

I began to want to know this companion better, asking him questions and speaking to him as a friend. But he only smiled and gave no answer. Sometimes I stubbornly pressed him for an answer, reaching out to place my hand on his shoulder, hoping it might bring us closer. But he easily moved away and said to me, “If we are to grow any closer, let me be the one to touch you. When that day comes, I will reach out my hand to greet you, and you will take it.” Those were the only words he ever spoke to me.

We all have this quiet companion walking beside us throughout our lives. His name is Death. He is not a cruel enemy, but a reminder that never leaves us. If we open our hearts to his presence and walk beside him without resistance or fear, we will begin to live each day with greater awareness and care, and to feel the true value of every breath we still have. It is this companion who helps us see how beautiful and brief every moment, every love, and every memory truly is—even though he hardly says a word along the way.
