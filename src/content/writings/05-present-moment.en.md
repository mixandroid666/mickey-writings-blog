Knowledge and technology have moved so far ahead in our time. They show how human ability grows faster with each passing day, like interest building upon interest. The aim of all these things is to solve and improve the problems in our lives. One clear and very real result is the thing we call a smartphone.

This thing is enormously useful to humanity. It offers itself as a means of solving, easing, and improving all kinds of human problems. Along with it come social media and entertainment delivered straight to our faces.

Many people are hooked on these things to a frightening degree. I am one of them. When we watch ourselves, we find that we reach for this thing almost all the time, without even knowing it. Whenever we wake up, or before we go to sleep, we take it in. Is this a kind of addiction?

Worse still, we forget that we live with people around us and within a wider society. Sometimes this addiction is strong enough to cause harm. We lose our sense of when and where it is proper to use a phone. We use it while driving, which may lead to accidents and death. We neglect the people close to us, the people talking to us, and those sharing our table over food or drinks.

I have been through this shameful experience many times. Sometimes friends or people close to me want to see me, so we arrange to meet at a restaurant or someone’s home. When we meet, we greet one another with smiles and joy. But after a little conversation, the kind we make out of courtesy, they take out their phones and play with them. Sometimes it goes on so long that it is startling, as though they have forgotten I am right in front of them. I sometimes try to start a new topic to bring their attention back to the table. All I get is silence, because they seem not to hear me. Others answer slowly because they are busy replying to some message. Is this quality time? If this is how it is going to be, you could simply send me a message. There is no need to make all that effort to come and see me.

In truth, I am not so strongly against these things. But I place great value on what is right before me. When we have not seen each other for a long time, we are full of longing. We want to meet and talk. Is that not why we come together? We should be all the more aware of it.

That is why I try to hold back from using my phone as much as possible, depending on the situation and what is proper at that time. I am often told that I am hard to talk to and hard to reach in this wireless world. Why has this question come up? In earlier days, we were perfectly able to wait. But now that everything has become faster, do people assume that everyone must move just as fast? If something is urgent and important, they can call. If they do not, then what they are trying to reach me about is probably not a matter of life and death.

Still, I am lucky to have many friends who hold the same values. One friend from university would always turn off his internet connection during class or a conversation. Some friends keep their phones in their pockets throughout our time together. If they take them out, it is only for a few seconds. Conversations with these people are full of a hunger for things to share. They always have fresh ideas that leave an impression on me.

This is part of why I try to invite you to pay more attention to the present moment. Time that has passed cannot be brought back. Drink deeply from the stream of time as it flows through you, together with your surroundings and the person you are talking to. This is the only thing we can turn into good memories.

I wrote this on a large phone.
