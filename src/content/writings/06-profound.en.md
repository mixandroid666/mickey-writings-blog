When I was young, I found that discovering new things was wonderfully exciting. A sight I had never seen, a sound I had never heard, or even a taste I had never tried. Our interest was full of questions and driven by a great force. Perhaps it was because we had no burdens to worry about yet. Life still had room for experiences to fill it and color it in. And we shaped the world of our senses freely and with joy.

One experience that brought me great ‘pleasure’ and ‘joy’ was discovering the thing called ‘music.’

Music takes sound waves of different frequencies and arranges them, weaves them together, and lets them blend. When this is done just right, a beautiful balance comes into being. It becomes the ‘sweetness of sound’ we feel when those waves reach our ears.

This arrangement of notes came to be called a ‘chord.’ At its most basic, it means playing at least three notes of different pitches together. I find this both mysterious and deeply curious. We can add a third note, a fourth, or more, and each addition gives us a different feeling in a wonderful way. It is as though a kind of happiness has been born. A delicate happiness, one that needs a vessel deep enough to receive it before we can reach it.

And it is not only music. Other experiences bring similar feelings: taking a plane for the first time, watching a good film, having a wish come true, or even winning.

Yet as I grow older, I find that the ‘vessel’ that holds these feelings needs something ‘higher’ than what came before. Perhaps it is like a kind of addiction: needing more, needing it stronger, and needing it more often.

So a question arises: what can fill this empty space?

Sometimes the answer seems not to lie in the world we can touch, but in something ‘beyond the world.’ Many spiritual ideas offer themselves, saying, ‘I am your answer.’ Each is like an item in a shop, lined up and waiting for us to choose.

Something else I discovered later is that this is not only about receiving things through the senses. It is also the process of ‘knowing’ that grows out of ‘not knowing.’

There is more that we do not know than we could ever count, compared with what we know. And the desire to know seems to have no end. The more we experience, the more questions spring up inside us, like mushrooms in a damp forest. They are complex, and sometimes they have no answer.

Some may be the very same questions that people asked thousands of years ago:
‘Where do we come from?’
‘Where do we go when we die?’
‘Who made the universe?’
‘Where does the universe end?’
‘What is the smallest thing?’
‘What is the largest thing?’

I long to know. I wonder. And I want answers.

I want to step across the line of ‘not knowing’ to fill something within my heart. For not knowing is like thorns that keep piercing me inside, without end.

This is only a piece of writing in which I grumble and reflect the truth of my own thoughts.
