The experience I speak of here is not the kind that tells us someone is skilled or well versed in a certain field. I mean meeting something new, something we have never met before. I bring this up because I see great value in new experiences. We can meet each of them for the first time only once in our lives. Meeting new people, hearing new songs, tasting new food, and taking in stories through one of the most wonderful things human beings have made: ‘films.’

Films are wonderful. They bring together art and knowledge from many fields, woven into a story by a skilled director. Such people deserve great praise. I admire them. How long must they spend gathering all those materials? And that is not all. They must bring things together, take them apart, write them down, and turn all these stories into a film for us to hear and see. Sometimes a film hides its meaning in ways that are both mysterious and sharp. The viewer must put in real thought to reach that meaning. Some simply scold the film, curse it, and cast it aside because they cannot understand it. I think this is part of the challenge, and a good measure of the director’s and the team’s ability: what we call ‘brains’ or ‘seasoned skill.’

Today we see all kinds of online media making content that spoils films. As far as I know, the English word ‘spoiler’ means revealing important parts of a film. Some people consume these spoilers like mad. Some go so far as to refuse to watch the film at all. Once they know the story, they proudly announce it to their friends: ‘I know this one already. Do you want to hear it? Then you won’t have to pay to see it and waste your money and time.’ Worse still, they blurt out the spoiler when the listener never wanted to hear it. This is one way of hurting someone.

We can meet a new experience only once in our lives. Let each person enjoy it for themselves. Spoiling a film is little different from killing it and leaving it to writhe pitifully. Think of how much work and money the makers and their team must spend before a single film can come to life. A viewer might find a reflection, or a ‘meaning,’ in that ‘fresh first experience’ of a film. It might even change their life. Do you see? A spoiler does not only kill the film itself. It also kills a chance for people who had no idea what was coming.

Please avoid watching spoilers and spoiling films. Avoid doing the same to other things too, if you can, such as songs and novels. Think of it as kindness toward all living beings. Honor not knowing, and gladly meet new experiences for yourselves. This is life, after all: a life we cannot know we will have again in another birth. Experience is sacred and wonderful. I ask you to understand this.
